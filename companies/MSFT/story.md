**What it does:** Microsoft rents Microsoft 365, Windows commercial licenses, Dynamics and LinkedIn to businesses around the world by the seat, and rents out computing power through Azure. FY2026 revenue was $331.8 billion, with an operating margin of 46.8% [src:MSFT-RPT1-2026-09-17#p3].

**Why it is a good business:** In the seat business, nearly sixty cents of every dollar of revenue is operating profit, and a customer who wants to replace it has to migrate its files, reconnect identity and rebuild compliance [src:MSFT-RPT1-2026-09-17#p3]. Companies buy Azure through the same contract and the same salesperson; Azure's full-year revenue passed $100 billion for the first time [src:MSFT-RPT1-2026-09-17#p3].

**What has to go right:** For the first time, it is putting the cash its annuity has piled up into data centers on a large scale: FY2026 capex was $115.9 billion, 34.9% of revenue, and free cash flow was down to just 52% of non-GAAP net income [src:MSFT-RPT1-2026-09-17#p5]. The spending has to buy profitable growth, the capex cycle has to come down, and the $678 billion backlog has to be backed by customers other than OpenAI, where the company's latest disclosure put a single customer at 45% (FY26 Q2, of the $625 billion total at the time) [src:MSFT-RPT1-2026-09-17#p8].

**What would prove me wrong:** Azure constant-currency growth below 20% for two straight quarters while capex is still above 25% of revenue, FY2028 cash conversion below 45%, Microsoft Cloud gross margin below 58% for two straight quarters, RPO growth excluding OpenAI below 5% or the $250 billion commitment restructured or impaired, or seats starting to fall while the company cannot show growing agent or usage revenue [src:MSFT-RPT1-2026-09-17#p11] [src:MSFT-RPT1-2026-09-17#p12]. Three more have to be read from documents: Nadella leaving with no disclosed succession plan, or the 2027 proxy's long-term incentives still lacking a return-on-capital measure while capex is still above $150 billion [src:MSFT-RPT1-2026-09-17#p12]; an impairment of Microsoft's OpenAI stake, or OpenAI unable to fund its compute commitments; and, once the EU's Cloud and AI Development Act takes effect, the Level 3 third-country recognition route closed to US providers in practice [src:MSFT-RPT1-2026-09-17#p12].
