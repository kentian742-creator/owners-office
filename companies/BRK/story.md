**What it does.** Berkshire pools the premiums its insurance customers pay in advance and the money its subsidiaries earn in Omaha, where headquarters decides where it goes: on 30 June 2026 it had about $177.5 billion of float [src:BRK-10Q-FY2026Q2#p38], $323.8 billion of stocks and $364.7 billion of cash and Treasury bills [src:BRK-10Q-FY2026Q2#p2], and its 2025 letter counted 51 non-insurance operating businesses in railways, power, manufacturing, retail and more [src:BRK-LTR-2025#p11]. It is a Lynch stalwart: from 2015 to 2025, after-tax operating earnings on the company's basis grew from $17.4 billion to $44.5 billion [src:BRK-10K-FY2015#p32] [src:BRK-LTR-2025#p8].

**Why it is a good business.** As long as underwriting does not lose money, float is negative-cost money with no maturity date that cannot be run on; underwriting has been profitable in each of the past three years [src:BRK-10K-FY2025#pK-35], and the 2025 combined ratio was 87.1% [src:BRK-LTR-2025#p9]. The float, the operating businesses and the equity portfolio are three uncorrelated lines of profit [src:BRK-RPT1-2026-09-18#p2]. Headquarters sets no goals or targets, most non-insurance businesses carry no debt, and subsidiary CEOs run their own businesses [src:BRK-LTR-2025#p11]; leverage, where used, comes from the centre: at the end of 2025 the parent guaranteed about $20.1 billion of subsidiary debt, mostly funding Clayton Homes' loans and Marmon's railcar leasing, while BNSF and BHE borrow on their own [src:BRK-10K-FY2025#pK-119] [src:BRK-10K-FY2025#pK-55].

**What must go right.** In a downturn, the new CEO has to turn cash into productive assets that earn more rather than leave that $364.7 billion earning Treasury-bill interest for years [src:BRK-10Q-FY2026Q2#p2] [src:BRK-RPT1-2026-09-18#p16]; GEICO has to restore retention without giving up underwriting discipline, after policies in force grew about 2% in the year to March 2026 (the CEO at the annual meeting, not in any filing), against 10.9% for Progressive's personal auto policies [src:BRK-NEWS-2026-05-04-CARRIERMANAGEMENT] [src:PGR-10Q-FY2026Q1#p33].

**What would prove me wrong.** GEICO's policies in force shrinking for two years in a row with a combined ratio above 100%, the group combined ratio above 100% for two years in a row, cash piled high at two consecutive year-ends while less than half of the period's operating cash flow was deployed over those two years, or the parent company guaranteeing or putting money into PacifiCorp [src:BRK-RPT1-2026-09-18#p14].
