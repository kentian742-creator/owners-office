**What it does.** McDonald's is paid mainly as a landlord and brand owner. About 95% of its 46,028 restaurants at 2026-06-30 are run by franchisees, licensees and affiliates [src:MCD-10Q-FY2026Q2#p16], who pay rent and royalties as a percentage of their sales [src:MCD-10K-FY2025#p4]. In FY2025 franchised restaurants produced about 90% of restaurant margin [src:MCD-10K-FY2025#p15].

**Why it is a good business.** Operators carry most of the operating cost, so in FY2025 the company earned a 46.1% operating margin [src:MCD-10K-FY2025#p18] and reported after-tax ROIC of 20.3% [src:MCD-10K-FY2025#p22]. For conventional franchises it generally controls the land and building [src:MCD-10K-FY2025#p3]. It has paid a dividend for 50 consecutive years and raised it every year [src:MCD-10K-FY2025#p21]. Growth comes from new restaurants, most of them funded by licensees, and from comparable sales [src:MCD-10K-FY2025#p11].

**What must go right.** First, U.S. customers must come back more often. U.S. growth has come from higher checks, and U.S. comparable guest counts were negative in FY2026Q2 [src:MCD-10Q-FY2026Q2#p19]. The U.S. company-operated restaurant margin, the nearest disclosed proxy for franchisee economics, fell from 15.2% in FY2023 [src:MCD-10K-FY2025#p14][src:MCD-10K-FY2025#p15] to 10.0% in FY2026Q1–Q2 [src:MCD-10Q-FY2026Q2#p24][src:MCD-10Q-FY2026Q2#p27]. Second, the larger building program must pay: capital spending rose from $2,357M in FY2023 to $3,365M in FY2025 while operating income rose $746M [src:MCD-10K-FY2025#p42][src:MCD-10K-FY2025#p39]. Third, dividends and buybacks must stay funded by free cash flow, not new debt.

**What would prove it wrong.** Global comparable sales below zero for two straight years; the share of U.S. franchised sales kept as revenue (14.2% in FY2025 [src:MCD-10K-FY2025#p14][src:MCD-10K-FY2025#p15]) falling below 13%; company-reported ROIC below 15% for two straight years [src:MCD-10K-FY2025#p22]; any cut in the dividend; or a debt rating below BBB or Baa2.
